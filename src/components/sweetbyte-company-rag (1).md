## 2. What Sweetbyte does

Sweetbyte is an Essex-based managed IT services provider (MSP) serving small and medium
businesses. It positions itself as a complete outsourced IT department rather than a break-fix
supplier: IT support, cyber security, cloud, networking and Wi-Fi, backup and disaster recovery,
VoIP telephony and CCTV — plus domain hosting, website design, marketing and business automation.

The central commercial differentiator is **no long-term lock-in contracts**: clients pay monthly
and can leave without penalty. Secondary differentiators are speed of response, a local Essex
engineering team, and having every service under one roof so clients avoid juggling vendors.

---

## 3. Positioning and differentiators

Five claims carry Sweetbyte's marketing. Lead with these in any client-facing copy.

1. **No long-term contracts.** Flexible monthly plans or pay-as-you-go. No lock-in periods, no
   early termination fees, cancel anytime. The top brochure tier runs as a three-month rolling
   contract. This is the most-repeated message in all material and should usually lead.
2. **Speed.** Over 90% of helpdesk calls resolved within the first 30 minutes. 98.8% SLA
   adherence.
3. **Local Essex team.** Own engineers, fast on-site attendance, no offshore call centre —
   callers speak directly to a team member.
4. **Longevity and experience.** Trading since 2005, with 25+ years of combined team IT
   experience (quoted as 40+ years on the network and CCTV pages). 40+ Google reviews at
   4.9 stars, and several clients retained 8–10+ years.
5. **Everything under one roof.** IT support, cyber, cloud, VoIP, backup, networking and CCTV
   from one provider — saving the client time and money versus multiple vendors.

**Target customer.** SMEs — from 5-person start-ups to companies of 50–100 staff. Sectors named
for CCTV work: retail, commercial, education, healthcare, warehouses, manufacturing.

**Trial offer.** "Try Us Before You Buy Us" — free consultation or free site visit, no obligation
(terms apply).

## 6. Service catalogue

### 6.1 IT Support
https://sweetbyte.co.uk/it-support-essex/

Three routes to market:
- **PAYASGO** — pay-as-you-go support, as and when needed, no contract.
- **IT Packages** — pay-monthly unlimited support tiers (see §7).
- **Bespoke IT** — custom requirements, scoped individually.

Support is split into **reactive** (break/fix, incident response, user requests, troubleshooting,

include GDPR documentation.

Work types covered across the tiers: remote support, on-site support, hardware repairs, OS
reloads, emergency loan equipment, relocations, new installs, upgrades, migrations.

## 9. Brand and voice guide

**Name.** Always "Sweetbyte". The logo renders it lowercase with "byte" in bold, preceded by a
circular spiral/target icon. Never capitalise the B in running text.

**Colours:**
- Deep navy — headings and emphasis
- Cyan / bright blue — primary accent, links, highlights
- Mid grey — secondary rule
- Green — used sparingly, for the "Green Innovation" and sustainability angle
- White backgrounds with circuit-trace line-art motifs

**Tone.** Plain-English, friendly, non-patronising, anti-jargon. The core promise is that IT is
complicated and Sweetbyte makes it simple. Copy is written in the first person plural ("we"),
addresses the reader as "you", and closes sections with a call to action — a phone number or a
"Contact Us" link.

**Recurring rhetorical moves worth reusing:**
- Open on the client's pain, framed as a question — "Fed up with slow IT support?",
  "Do you have an anti-virus solution?"
- Contrast against other MSPs — "Unlike most IT companies…"
- Reassure that they won't reach a call centre.
- Close on the phone number in large type.

**Spelling.** UK English throughout: specialises, optimisation, defence, modernise, organisation.

**Guardrails.** Don't use a statistic that isn't in §3 or §7, a client name that isn't in §8, or
a surname for any team member other than Sweetman. In formal or contractual documents, soften
absolute claims ("100% accuracy", "100% coverage guarantee") and attribute typical-results
figures as typical rather than guaranteed.
